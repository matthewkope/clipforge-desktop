## ⚠️ Important — read this first

**These documents are templates, not legal advice.** They were drafted to give a
solo/independent developer a reasonable baseline that follows the common pattern
used by open-source media-downloader tools (disclaim warranties, cap liability,
push responsibility for downloaded content onto the user, and stay neutral as a
general-purpose tool).

They **reduce** legal risk; they do **not** make it impossible to be sued. No
document can do that — anyone can file a lawsuit. What good policies do is:

1. Make clear the tool is general-purpose and that the user — not you — chooses
   what to download and is responsible for having the rights to do so.
2. Disclaim warranties and cap your financial liability.
3. Give a clear takedown / contact process so disputes can be resolved without
   litigation.

**Before you publish or distribute ClipForge, especially commercially, have a
licensed attorney in your jurisdiction review these.** Key things a lawyer
should weigh in on:

- Whether distributing a tool that can circumvent platform access controls
  raises issues under the DMCA's anti-circumvention provisions (17 U.S.C. §1201)
  or your local equivalent, and the platforms' Terms of Service.
- Whether you need a registered business entity (LLC, etc.) to put liability on
  the company instead of you personally — **a liability cap in Terms is far less
  protective if "you" and "the company" are the same natural person.**
- Consumer-protection and contract-formation rules in the regions where you have
  users (an enforceable "clickwrap" acceptance, etc.).
- DMCA designated-agent registration if you operate any hosted service.
